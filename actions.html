Government Actions Protecting Endangered Animals

Governments use protected areas, anti-poaching enforcement, habitat restoration and international wildlife agreements to protect threatened species. These measures can protect animals, but they are most effective when local communities share in the benefits.

Tiger protection

  • India's Project Tiger began in 1973 and established the network of tiger reserves now overseen by the National Tiger Conservation Authority.
  • India's Wildlife Protection Act provides legal protection and penalties for the hunting and trade of protected wildlife.
  • Tiger-range governments use forest and wildlife agencies to carry out population monitoring, anti-poaching patrols, and habitat and corridor protection under national tiger action plans.
  • CITES controls regulate international trade in tigers and tiger parts, with implementation by participating governments.

Orangutan protection

  • Indonesia's National Strategy and Action Plan coordinates orangutan habitat protection, population monitoring and conflict reduction.
  • Indonesia has established protected forests and national parks in important orangutan habitats, including Leuser and Tanjung Puting.
  • Palm-oil moratoriums and land-use controls reduce new clearing of primary forests and peatlands, helping prevent further habitat loss.
  • Wildlife rescue, rehabilitation and release programmes are overseen by Indonesian and Malaysian environmental authorities.

Rhinoceros protection

  • Nepal and India protect rhinos through national parks, regular monitoring, anti-poaching units and habitat management.
  • South Africa's National Environmental Management: Biodiversity Act and conservation authorities regulate rhino trade, ownership and protection.
  • Protected sanctuaries and wildlife reserves provide secure habitat and allow rhinos to be moved into new or safer areas.
  • CITES Appendix I trade restrictions, enforced by participating governments, make international commercial trade in most rhino species illegal.
