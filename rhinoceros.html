Rhinoceros

Rhinocerotidae

Rhinoceroses are large herbivores with tough skin and one or two horns. They shape grasslands and forests by grazing, spreading seeds and creating water and wallowing sites used by other animals. Rhino species remain vulnerable to poaching for illegal horn trade and to the loss of habitat across Africa and Asia.

Recent population statistics

About 15,752 southern white rhinos, the largest surviving population
About 6,487 black rhinos estimated in the wild
About 4,014 greater one-horned rhinos estimated in the wild
Only about 76 Javan rhinos remain in the wild
Only 34-47 Sumatran rhinos are estimated to remain

Latest widely published estimates: IUCN African and Asian Rhino Specialist Groups and the International Rhino Foundation, 2023-2024.
